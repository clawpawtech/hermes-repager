# Competitive review: from guarded generator to a design decision lab

Baseline reviewed: **8b8e3a7d99199801d8d63ff4d7275b12fed90b12**. Research date: **2026-11-11**. Goal: a materially more useful and credible top-10 contender, not a predicted placement.

## Method and limits

Read the official hackathon announcement, current plugin/catalog/LLM documentation, selected marketplace entries and three pinned source entrypoints. Two independent, read-only Codex reviewers examined the baseline; one focused on product/design, one on reliability/security. The parent separately inspected actual native screenshots and reproduced issues. A later independent technical review checked the new work and found integration mismatches, which were fixed with exact-boundary regressions.

These references are **quality benchmarks and adjacent products, not a verified ranking or list of hackathon finalists**. Hosted competitors were not installed, signed into or exercised. Their descriptions are attributed claims, not our performance measurements. Source inspection was scoped, not a security certification. No competitor application code or assets were copied.

The Impeccable context/detector scripts were attempted but absent from the installed skill; no detector pass is claimed. Independent reviews, real screenshots, code probes and browser tests provide the evidence instead. No novice first-use study or physical Android-device test has been completed.

## Baseline verdict

**Strong integrity intent, narrower assurance than the experience implied, and insufficient decision value.** The baseline was already much more than a prompt: guarded Chromium, explicit scope, host-model integration, durable artifacts and revision-bound receipts were real. But a safely generated text layout did not explain why a person should choose it over an already polished source site.

Reproduced weaknesses:

- An all-white foreground/background/button combination was accepted and could pass the old 13 checks. Geometry and nonempty DOM text are not contrast.
- Approval metadata itself was unsigned; editing its source IDs could be legitimized by a newly signed revision. This was a **local-record integrity gap**, not a demonstrated remote website-to-filesystem exploit.
- Current export could carry tampered historical metadata, and replaying an older record/receipt could defeat record-only stale-export expectations.
- A visible navigation heading could end SPA readiness waiting before the real supported hero appeared. Fully covered elements could still enter the evidence scope.
- Free-route checks missed router aliases and ambiguous inherited configuration.
- Two sequential model-selected templates, collapsed approval copy, technical provenance text and weak mobile/reconnect guidance created ceremony without enough practical choice.
- The exported ZIP was technically useful but required a person to interpret raw metadata and locate screenshots. It lacked a client-readable decision document.

No conversion defect, full-site repair need or visual superiority of our proposal was established for GetFluid. Its source is already polished and intentionally remains untouched.

## What changed, by the actual judging criteria

### Usefulness

- Four authored directions and seven direct presentation controls, with **no additional inference** for local remixes.
- Readable differences from the parent revision; historical restore creates a fresh revision under the same approved contract.
- Exact source-region highlights, canonical inspection, mobile-first evidence and explicit copy/eligibility guidance.
- Prefer/reject/undecided plus a reason tied to the exact checked artifact. Failed checked candidates can be rejected, not exported or preferred.
- A self-contained visual HTML report, embedded real before/after images, actual checks, limitations, choice and file checksums in the ZIP.

### Reliability

- Signed approval and source/scope equality checks before generation, verification or export.
- Authenticated current-record head and crash-recoverable before/after journal, with the full-filesystem rollback boundary documented.
- Historical metadata and retained HTML validation, immutable revisions and strict current-receipt gates.
- Readability constraints plus **29 protocol-2 browser checks**, including exact observed copy and computed contrast. Legacy/incomplete receipts cannot authorize a current handoff.
- Bounded supported-scope readiness and occlusion tests; no source-specific fallback or guard weakening.
- Explicit task provider/model, empty canonical fallback lanes, known free-alias refusal, and returned-route audit. No price or host-transport guarantee inferred from a string.
- Producer-to-canvas schema and exact restore-payload regressions, not just matching independent mocks.

### Polish

- More deliberate native hierarchy: observe, adjust, decide; result-first presentation, visible unknown counts, source/contract context instead of raw IDs alone.
- Exact approval copy open by default, preserved drafts/consent/focus, collapsed saved reviews on phone and less duplicate capture UI.
- Active-job discovery precedes potentially locked gallery reads. Errors distinguish connection loss from unsupported source content.
- Original repager branding remains self-contained and plugin-owned. No host-shell skinning or extra runtime fonts.
- A human-readable handoff rather than a JSON scavenger hunt.

### Creativity

The differentiator is **evidence-constrained art direction**, not four templates by themselves: freeze the approved message, vary only presentation, verify the actual bytes and preserve the reason for choosing a version. This deliberately sacrifices arbitrary generation breadth for a decision that can be inspected and handed off.

## Re-review outcomes and remaining limits

The independent re-review reproduced Restore's empty-change payload being rejected by the service, and a UI/server legacy-receipt mismatch. Both were fixed at the boundary, not hidden in the client. It also identified useful refinements: failed-candidate rejection and canonical fallback types, now implemented.

Still outside the claim: full-site reconstruction, copied imagery, integration-ready framework patches, comprehensive accessibility, complete occlusion proof, production deployment, automatic retention/cancellation, external monotonic attestation, novice usability preference, physical Android playback and an actual contest ranking. The source-versus-proposal comparison uses equal viewports but **different scope**.
